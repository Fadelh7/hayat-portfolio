# Finding Inspiration in Everyday Life

As artists, we often search for grand moments of inspiration—the breathtaking landscape, the profound emotional experience, the revolutionary idea. But some of my most meaningful work has emerged from paying attention to the ordinary, everyday aspects of life that we typically overlook.

## The Beauty in Mundane Objects

There is something magical about finding beauty in objects we encounter every day. A worn coffee cup, a well-used kitchen table, a pair of old shoes—these items carry stories and histories. They bear the marks of human touch and the passage of time.

In my still life work, I often focus on these mundane objects, elevating them through careful observation and representation. By paying attention to the play of light on a simple glass or the subtle color variations in a piece of fruit, I invite viewers to see the extraordinary in the ordinary.

## Rituals and Routines

Our daily rituals and routines can also be rich sources of inspiration. The morning cup of coffee, the evening walk, the preparation of a meal—these repeated actions form the rhythm of our lives.

I've created several series exploring these everyday rituals, particularly those traditionally associated with women's work. By highlighting these often-invisible forms of labor, I aim to honor the quiet dignity of these activities and question why they have been historically undervalued.

## Urban Landscapes

Living in Beirut, I'm surrounded by a constantly evolving urban landscape. Construction sites, street corners, neighborhood shops—these everyday scenes contain countless stories and visual possibilities.

I often sketch while walking through the city, capturing fleeting moments and ordinary scenes. These sketches sometimes develop into larger works, but even when they don't, the practice of observation helps me stay connected to my surroundings and attuned to the visual richness of everyday life.

## Human Interactions

Some of my most compelling work has been inspired by observing everyday human interactions—a mother and child at a bus stop, friends chatting in a café, strangers navigating a crowded street.

These moments of connection, conflict, or coexistence reveal so much about human nature and social dynamics. By capturing these interactions in my work, I aim to create a kind of visual sociology that honors the complexity of everyday life.

## Finding Your Own Inspiration

If you're an artist struggling to find inspiration, I encourage you to look more closely at your everyday life. Here are some practices that have helped me:

1. **Keep a visual journal**: Document interesting objects, scenes, or moments you encounter throughout your day.
2. **Practice mindful observation**: Take time to really look at familiar objects or environments, noticing details you might normally overlook.
3. **Change your routine**: Take a different route to work, shop at a new market, or visit a neighborhood you rarely frequent.
4. **Collect interesting objects**: Create a collection of items that catch your eye, even if you're not sure why they interest you.
5. **Listen to stories**: Pay attention to the stories people tell about their everyday lives—these narratives often contain rich material for artistic exploration.

## Conclusion

Inspiration doesn't always announce itself with fanfare. Often, it whispers to us through the objects, routines, and interactions that make up our daily lives. By cultivating attention and curiosity toward the everyday, we can discover endless possibilities for creative expression.

As the poet Mary Oliver wrote, "Pay attention. Be astonished. Tell about it." This simple formula has guided my artistic practice and continues to reveal new sources of inspiration in the most unexpected places.

Remember that art doesn't have to be about grand themes or extraordinary experiences. Sometimes, the most powerful work comes from illuminating the beauty, complexity, and significance of ordinary life—helping others see the magic that exists in the mundane.
